Singing Voice Conversion (SVC)

SoulX-Singer-SVC converts a source singing recording into the target singer's voice while preserving the original melody, rhythm, and lyrics. Unlike the SVS pipeline, SVC works directly from raw audio — no lyric transcription or MIDI notes are required.

How It Works

SVC fine-tunes SoulX-Singer's generative capability on an audio-to-audio conversion objective. The model:

  1. Extracts the pitch (F0) and content from the source audio.
  2. Extracts the timbre from the target singer prompt.
  3. Synthesizes new audio combining the source content/pitch with the target timbre.

This is fully transcription-free — no intermediate text or MIDI representation is used.

Limitations

  • Music bleed — If the source audio contains heavy background music that is not fully separated, it may bleed into the output. Use a high-quality vocal separator or a clean dry vocal as input.
  • Extreme pitch ranges — Conversion quality degrades if the source pitch range is far outside the target singer's natural range.
  • Language — SVC is language-agnostic, but very language-specific vocal characteristics (e.g., tonal phonemes in Mandarin) may be imperfectly preserved when converting across languages.
  • Style — Unusual vocal styles (extreme vibrato, growling, falsetto) may not transfer cleanly.

Tips for Best Results

  • Use a clean, isolated vocal for both source and prompt. Vocal separation (preprocess.sh) helps, but a dry studio recording is ideal.
  • Keep the prompt recording to 5–15 seconds — enough to capture stable timbre without over-fitting to a specific phrase.
  • Match the pitch range of source to target where possible. Shifting pitch by more than an octave degrades quality.
  • For cross-lingual conversion (e.g., Mandarin source → English-accented timbre), expect some accent mixing — this is a known limitation.
